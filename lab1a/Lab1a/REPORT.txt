1. OBJECTIVE / ARCHITECTURE
The lab uses the supplied synthetic logs and a deterministic detector:
logs/security.log -> detector (5s rescan) -> output/findings.json -> dashboard (:8000).
Local STIX data enriches rule matches with MITRE ATT&CK metadata. No real attacks
or network scans were carried out. The detector is rule-based, not AI/LLM.

3. EVIDENCE -> RULE -> TECHNIQUE -> TACTIC -> UNCERTAINTY
T1110 / Brute Force / Credential Access:
Evidence: 3 failed-password lines from 172.18.0.5.
Rule: at least 3 failed password events grouped by one source IP.
Uncertainty: no proof of malicious intent or successful access.

T1046 / Network Service Discovery / Discovery:
Evidence: one explicit network-scan notice from 172.18.0.7.
Rule: line contains 'network scan detected'.
Uncertainty: no independent packet-level evidence of a real scan.

6. SOC NARRATIVE (126 WORDS)
On 4 November 2026, I tested a rule-based SOC detector in Docker on Pop!_OS. The clean six-line log produced two findings: T1110 (High) after three failed SSH password events from 172.18.0.5, and T1046 (Medium) for an explicit scan notice from 172.18.0.7. In C1, removing one failure eliminated T1110. In C2, distributing failures among three source IPs also avoided the threshold. In C3, three failures from 192.0.2.55 created a new T1110. In C4, a scan notice from 192.0.2.77 added T1046, giving three findings. A later simulator screenshot showed a new T1046 for 172.18.0.31. These observations confirm how the implemented rules classify log text; they do not establish hostile intent, a real scan, successful authentication by an attacker, or compromise. Corroboration would require independent endpoint and network evidence.

7. LIMITATIONS AND CLEANUP
- T1110 can miss distributed or below-threshold attempts and has no time window.
- T1046 merely matches explicit scan text, not packet/flow evidence.
- MITRE mapping indicates consistency with behavior, not confirmed compromise.
